0:35-1:20 The problem is simple: raw video gives visibility, but it does not give incident memory. During an incident, the Ops Centre may receive several live feeds at once. Someone still has to watch those feeds, notice what changed, remember which responder saw it, and decide whether it matters operationally. That is a lot to ask in a live situation. Important visible cues can be missed, repeated over radio, or buried in handwritten notes. After the incident, teams still have to rebuild the timeline from memory, radio logs, and scattered evidence. So the gap is not camera access. SCDF can already move in that direction. The harder gap is turning what officers see into source-linked, confirmed operational facts.

2:00-2:45 Here is how 1stSight works. A phone prototype simulates a responder bodycam. It sends sampled frames with basic incident metadata: source, time, approximate position, responder ID, and incident ID. For the prototype, `gpt-5.4-mini` handles the first pass on the sampled frames. It looks for visible scene cues that may deserve review. Each cue stays tied to its exact source frame, confidence score, timestamp, and location context. The important part is the human decision point. Ops Centre officers confirm, dismiss, or annotate the cue. AI does not issue commands. It does not diagnose patients. It does not make automated command decisions. Once officers confirm an observation, `deepseek-v4-pro` helps consolidate it into the live timeline, handover notes, after-action material, and exportable incident record. AI suggests. Officers confirm.

3:40-4:25 Here is one example. In a medical response, the model may suggest that stretcher access is constrained because of crowding near a corridor. At this point, it is only an AI cue for review. 1stSight can pin that cue to the scene map automatically. The Ops Centre officer then inspects the source frame and approves, rejects, or edits it before it becomes a confirmed event. If the officer confirms it, the event enters the incident timeline and handover summary with the original frame attached. The boundary matters. 1stSight does not perform medical diagnosis, facial recognition, or automated command. It packages visible evidence so humans can verify it faster and more consistently.

4:25-5:00 Technically, this is simple enough to prototype, but structured enough to deploy properly. Capture can begin as a mobile PWA that simulates a responder bodycam. The backend ingests sampled frames and metadata, including incident ID, responder source, timestamp, and approximate location. `gpt-5.4-mini` handles the visual cue extraction from sampled bodycam frames. `deepseek-v4-pro` handles the text-side analysis: timeline consolidation, handover summary, and report drafting from confirmed records. The record layer stores source frames and officer actions. The UI then presents the map pins, timeline, handover notes, and report export. The web app and backend services can run on Dell Cloud Native Platform. The model calls sit behind the backend, so the Ops Centre workflow does not have to change if SCDF later changes the approved model provider.
